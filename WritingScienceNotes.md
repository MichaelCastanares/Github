Notes on Writing Science book by Joshua Schimel

# The Challenge
- Important: Having a question (key) + hypothesis (support)
- Difference between information collected ("Our objective were") versus the knowledge hope to gain ("Our question was")
- Ask the question. Encourage Curiousity
- Map out the approach you took (Objectives, Methods)
- Some telegraph the conclusion, "In this paper we show ..."

# Characteristics of Good challenges:
    - to learn X, we did Y
# Bad Challenges
    - focuses on the information rather than knowledge
    - "we did Y" only



# The Funnel: Connecting Opening to Challenge
- Show what we don't know and why it is important
- Bizzwidget problem: (1) Open with a concern that people share (2) show why we need a solution, (3) introduce the product
- Explicitly define the problem
- Avoid: "Little is known about this topic" or "Our objectives were to carry out the following tasks"
- Good introduction: Defines a problem and narrows to an interesting question
- Poor introduction: failes to define a problem/sell a solution before defining the problem
- drawing and framing the knowlege gap to SUCCES formula
- Unexpected: I didn't know that
- Closing with a question: what is the answer?

# Story telling
- find the story that the data is telling
- hypothesis may not be the same as results. The story is why



# Structures of story
- SUCCES: simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, stories
- ABDCE: action, background, development, climax ending
- OCAR (typical): opening, challenge, action, resolution
- LDR: lead, development, resolution
- LD (fastest): lead, development


# Simple versus Simplistic
- Simple focuses on addressing the problem
- Simplistic is trivial


# OPENING
- Know your target audience
- Taylor your opening sentence to suit your audience
- Use the power of opening
- Two-step opening: make it quick or you stumble
- evaluate whether your opening overproise, on-target, or under-promise
